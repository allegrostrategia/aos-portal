## The report itself — how figures read

**1. A figure with nothing in it shows a dash, never a zero.**
A month where nobody entered a number and a month where the number really was nought are different facts, and a zero draws a bar on a chart that says something happened.
*If you'd rather see zeros:* every chart and panel would need to tell the two apart some other way, or stop trying. This one is woven through everything — it's the most expensive on the list to change.
**→ Agreed on 28 October. No change.**

**2. "Total hours spent delivering" counts as bad when it goes up.**
It's marked good-when-falling, so a month where you delivered more reads as a problem. Right for efficiency, wrong for a growing business.
*If you'd rather it were neutral or good-when-rising:* one line, and the sentence in the panel changes with it.
**→ Changed, and built.** It's neutral now: the figure and its change from last month, with no colour and nothing said about it in either panel. **What it sums, for the record:** the hours entered against each offer, added up across every offer that has *any* figure that month — units sold, revenue or hours. An offer set up but untouched that month isn't counted as nought, it's left out. If no offer has an hours figure at all, the total is a dash, not 0. It never reaches outside the Offers section: launch hours and trial-reel hours are their own figures.

**3. Every panel line ends with the same tail.** Three sentences in a row finishing "…not the direction we want, let's dig into WHY" read as templated rather than written.
*Recommended:* the tail on the first line only, the rest plain. Two other options are written up: two or three rotating versions you write, or the tail once under the heading. **Nothing is built either way** — this one is waiting on you.
**→ Changed, and built.** The tails are gone. A line going the wrong way now wears a small **FOCUS AREA** tag above it and says the rest plainly — "New clients dropped from 6 to 4". Your four celebration lines are untouched: "stunning", "& that's no accident", "WOOOO" and "exactly the direction we want" all stay as you wrote them — **you confirmed on 29 October that they stay.**

**4. A figure pulled from another section isn't said twice.**
"Revenue from offers" appears in Financials and comes from Offers. The panel used to mention both.
*If you'd rather it said both:* one line, but the panel gets repetitive.
**→ Agreed on 28 October. No change.**

**5. Two figures that move together say it once.** Issues raised and issues per ten clients are the same news twice, so only the plain count is said.
*If you'd rather both:* one line.
**→ Agreed on 28 October. No change.**

**6. A month's report is locked once published.**
Corrections go unpublish → fix → republish, which emails the client to say it changed.
*If you'd rather corrections were silent:* the email is one line to drop, but a client reading a changed figure wouldn't know it changed.
**→ Agreed on 28 October. No change.**

**7. A published month keeps the figures it went out with.**
Comparisons, targets, benchmarks and row names are frozen at publish, so a client re-reading January sees January as it was sent.
*If you'd rather it updated:* substantial work to undo, and a client's report would quietly change under them.
**→ Agreed on 28 October. No change.**

**8. Deleting an offer, funnel or campaign is refused if it has figures.**
"Retire" is the route — it comes off the entry screens and the history stays.
*If you'd rather delete outright:* it would take a client's past months with it. Strongly recommend keeping.
**→ Agreed on 28 October. No change.**

**9. Nothing is ever deleted** — cancelling revokes access and keeps every record.
This is a standing rule of the whole product, not a reporting decision.
**→ Agreed on 28 October. No change.**

## One left open, for later if you want it

**A margin target, so an offer's margin ring can go orange on its own account.**

The approved Offers mock-up draws a warning under a card reading *"Below your 45% margin target"*. **There is no margin target in the tool.** A client sets a target hourly rate and nothing else, so the warning — and the orange ring beside it — are drawn against that instead: an offer goes orange when its effective hourly rate falls under the rate you set, which is the same target the bar chart lower down the page is measured against.

That is a real test and it catches real problems, but it is not quite the same question. An offer can clear your hourly rate and still be thin once delivery costs come out of it, and a margin target would be the thing that says so.

*What it would take:* a new column on the client's settings, a field on the settings screen to fill it in, and one more comparison on the offer card. **It needs a database migration**, which is the only reason it is here rather than built — it is an hour's work, not a week's, but it changes the shape of the stored data and that is your call rather than Dom's.

*Nothing is built either way*, and nothing needs to be. The rings work as described above until you say otherwise.

## Does the report show whether each figure went up or down?

You asked for this, and the answer is **mostly yes — here is exactly where not**.

**Where it does.** Every KPI card carries it: the label, the figure, then *"↑ 18% vs. August"*. The arrow is the movement and the colour is the meaning — and the colour comes from the figure's own direction, never from the arrow, so a fall in costs is green and a rise in churn is red. That covers the six cards on the Overview and the headline cards at the top of every section. A month with nothing before it says *"No month to compare"* rather than drawing an arrow against nothing.

**Four places it doesn't, and why.**

1. **"Everything else this month"** — the list at the foot of a section, holding whatever didn't fit in the top six cards. Label and figure only. **This is the one worth changing if you want it**, and it is the only one of the four that is a choice rather than a fact: there is a previous figure for every line in it.
2. **The per-row tables** — each offer, each campaign, each funnel. No up-or-down per row. A row's history belongs to that offer or campaign, and what it needs is a small chart rather than an arrow; squeezing one more column into a six-column table on a phone is where this would go wrong.
3. **A launch** — a launch has no "last month". It is compared against *another launch*, on the Compare screen, which is yours only.
4. **The top three hooks and b-roll** — text and a view count, not a figure with a previous value.

**→ You said yes on 29 October, and it is built.** "Everything else this month" carries the arrows now, in the same style as the cards above it, drawn from the same map so a figure cannot be green in one place and red in the other. Neutral figures — the ones with no good direction, and the ones that didn't move — are grey. The month compared against is said once in the heading rather than on every row, because a dozen rows each saying "vs. August" is noise. There is a picture at `screens-review/11-overflow-arrows-client`.

The other three stay as they are: the per-row tables, a launch (which has no "last month"), and the top three hooks.
