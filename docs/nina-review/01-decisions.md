# Thirty-eight things decided for you

Nina — while the reporting tool was being built, thirty-eight questions came up that are really yours. Rather than stop each time, Dom picked an answer so the work could carry on, and every one was written down. This is that list.

**Nothing here is settled.** Each one says what was chosen, why, and what changes if you'd rather it went the other way. Most are one line to reverse. Where something would take real work to change, it says so.

They're grouped by the part of the product they belong to. Read the headings and stop where you disagree.

---

## The report itself — how figures read

**1. A figure with nothing in it shows a dash, never a zero.**
A month where nobody entered a number and a month where the number really was nought are different facts, and a zero draws a bar on a chart that says something happened.
*If you'd rather see zeros:* every chart and panel would need to tell the two apart some other way, or stop trying. This one is woven through everything — it's the most expensive on the list to change.

**2. "Total hours spent delivering" counts as bad when it goes up.**
It's marked good-when-falling, so a month where you delivered more reads as a problem. Right for efficiency, wrong for a growing business.
*If you'd rather it were neutral or good-when-rising:* one line, and the sentence in the panel changes with it.

**3. Every panel line ends with the same tail.** Three sentences in a row finishing "…not the direction we want, let's dig into WHY" read as templated rather than written.
*Recommended:* the tail on the first line only, the rest plain. Two other options are written up: two or three rotating versions you write, or the tail once under the heading. **Nothing is built either way** — this one is waiting on you.

**4. A figure pulled from another section isn't said twice.**
"Revenue from offers" appears in Financials and comes from Offers. The panel used to mention both.
*If you'd rather it said both:* one line, but the panel gets repetitive.

**5. Two figures that move together say it once.** Issues raised and issues per ten clients are the same news twice, so only the plain count is said.
*If you'd rather both:* one line.

**6. A month's report is locked once published.**
Corrections go unpublish → fix → republish, which emails the client to say it changed.
*If you'd rather corrections were silent:* the email is one line to drop, but a client reading a changed figure wouldn't know it changed.

**7. A published month keeps the figures it went out with.**
Comparisons, targets, benchmarks and row names are frozen at publish, so a client re-reading January sees January as it was sent.
*If you'd rather it updated:* substantial work to undo, and a client's report would quietly change under them.

**8. Deleting an offer, funnel or campaign is refused if it has figures.**
"Retire" is the route — it comes off the entry screens and the history stays.
*If you'd rather delete outright:* it would take a client's past months with it. Strongly recommend keeping.

**9. Nothing is ever deleted** — cancelling revokes access and keeps every record.
This is a standing rule of the whole product, not a reporting decision.

## What clients see, and when

**10. A client never sees a draft month.** Until you publish, there is nothing there for them.
*If you'd rather they saw progress:* possible, but they'd be reading half-finished figures.

**11. Publishing a month emails the client a link.** No figures in the email — just that it's ready.
*If you'd rather no email:* one line.

**12. Publishing a launch does *not* email them.** The launch page becomes visible and the next monthly report mentions it. One email a month rather than two.
*If you'd rather it did:* one line.

**13. "Compare launches" is yours only.** A client never sees this launch against their better one — that's a conversation you choose to have.
*If you'd rather clients had it:* one line.

**14. A client's reply to a month stays, once sent.** No editing, no deleting.
*If you'd rather they could:* a conversation that can be edited afterwards is hard to trust later.

**15. The Friday reflection never enters the shared chat.** Settled by you in September; listed here only so it's in one place.

## Launches

**16. The planner's first guesses are 47% show-up and 5% conversion** — the numbers from your own worked example, so its first answer matches one you've already sanity-checked. Editable.
*If you'd rather different defaults:* one line.

**17. A launch's status is set by hand.** Nothing moves it automatically, because dates slip and "finished" is a judgement about whether the cart is really shut.
*If you'd rather it followed the dates:* possible, and it would sometimes be wrong.

**18. A published launch's status can still change; nothing else about it can.** A client looking at "Live now" three weeks after the cart shut is worse served than one whose badge changed quietly. **Checked: status feeds no figure.**
*If you'd rather it froze too:* one line.

**19. Pitch retention is measured against who was there when the pitch began**, not when the session began. The brief asks for both figures and only spends one, so the unused one is probably what it meant.
*This changes a number you'd quote out loud, so it needs your word.*

**20. Conversion is measured against the main selling stage's attendees**, not everyone who attended anything. A launch with a waitlist and a challenge before the masterclass would otherwise read as converting a fraction of a far larger number.
*Listed because it looks wrong at a glance and is right.*

**21. A stage with one email shows its open rate on the dot**, so it reads as a figure rather than a stray mark.
*Alternative:* leave single-email stages off the chart and show them as a number beside it.

**22. A launch's cover image is optional**, and there's one per launch, replaced in place.
*If you'd rather several:* real work, and a gallery nobody asked for.

**23. A cover sits above the figures, full width.** The brief asks for one and never says where.
*Alternative:* small, beside the title.

**24. The monthly Launches tab lists launches whose live dates fall in that month**, as cards linking through — so a month says a launch happened without repeating its figures. A launch spanning two months appears in both.

**25. Sales by source that don't add up warn rather than refuse.** A real launch has sales nobody can attribute.

## aOS members reporting on themselves

**26. A member finds their report on "You", plus a card on Piazza while last month is unfinished.** Not a seventh navigation item — it's a once-a-month job, and a seventh item would be the most prominent thing on every screen for the twenty-eight days nobody needs it. **The card is private to that member** and never a post in the shared feed.
*If you'd rather a nav item:* one line, and the nav gets more crowded.

**27. "Core fields" are the ones already marked core in the field list**, seeded from your own section 5. Two consequences: a section with nothing set up (no funnels, no campaigns) counts as done rather than permanently orange, and **"clients at the start, when you joined" is asked once, not every month**.

**28. The core list is 44 figures, and four may be too many.** Every one must be filled before a section goes green, so the orange marker is only worth trusting if each is a number somebody would chase. *Recommended optional:* hours spent on trial reels, hours spent delivering per offer, revenue per ad campaign, and cash in bank.
*If you move them:* they stay on screen under "+ Add more detail", every calculation still works when they're filled, and the figures that depend on them already show a dash when they're not.

**29. Hiding a section is display-only.** Nothing is deleted, turning it back on brings everything back, and a figure pulled into another section keeps working while its own section is hidden.

**30. Reminders go on the 1st and the 8th, and nowhere else.** The 8th only if the month isn't done. Never to a cancelled member, never to a Chiarezza login past its end date, and the dates are UK time.

**31. Reminders go to aOS members only, not Chiarezza.** Your section 8.1 names only members, and it's also the only thing the system allows — a Chiarezza attendee isn't a member and the reminder queue is built on members.
*If you want attendees reminded:* real work, and the exclusion rule is already written for that day.

**32. Nobody is told when a member hasn't finished** except you, on the admin list, when you look.
*If you'd rather be notified:* one line, and it's one email per unfinished member per month.

**33. A member's reporting workspace is created with them**, so nobody can arrive and find nothing. It starts named after the person, because we don't hold a business name, and the setup screen is where they correct it.

**34. The panels say "we"**, which reads oddly when a member is reporting on themselves — "we didn't hit the target we wanted" is your voice talking to a client, and a member reading their own report is being addressed by nobody.
*Do members get a second set of wording?* Roughly a day of writing for you. **Not built either way.**

**35. A "Saving…" state on the save buttons.** A nicety, not a fix — the figures are never lost, only the confirmation, so somebody who clicks and leaves immediately never sees "Saved." *Recommended after launch.*

## Uploading files

**36. CSV upload is yours only, on retainer clients.** A member fills Social Media and Ads in by hand for now, although they're their own editor everywhere else. Every "my CSV didn't work" lands on you, and your time shouldn't scale with member count.
*The cost is honest:* the people who'd gain most from the shortcut don't get it. **One condition in one place to reverse.**

**37. A part-month file is refused.** A file covering the 1st to the 28th of September is refused for September — your own sample is exactly that. A month's spend filled from twenty-eight days of it is wrong by two days and says nothing about being wrong.
*The consequence:* **nobody can upload part-way through a month to see how it's going.** Re-exporting after the month ends is the only route.

**38. Campaigns are matched by name, exactly.** A campaign renamed in Meta reads as a new one and is offered as new. No fuzzy matching and no remembered renames — both are real conveniences and both are ways a month's spend quietly lands on the wrong campaign.
*Recommended as later work rather than never.*

## One thing that is not a decision, and is waiting for you

**A campaign with no figures for the month shows on the published report as a row of dashes.** That's true whether it came from a file or was typed in — a campaign set up in August and paused in September is a row of dashes on September's report.

Both answers are defensible: a dash says the campaign exists and did nothing, which is sometimes the point and sometimes noise. **Not changed, because it's the one thing on this list a client can already see.** Your call.
