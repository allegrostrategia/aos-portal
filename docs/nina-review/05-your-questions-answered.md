# Five things you asked about

Nina — these are the five questions from your review of the live portal, answered as the tool actually works today. Nothing here is a plan; it's a description. Where something can't be done yet, it says so and says what it would take.

---

## 1. Where "Set targets" goes, and who sets targets

**The button takes you to a single page listing every figure in every section that client has**, grouped the way the tabs are grouped, with two boxes beside each one: a **standing** target that carries on month after month, and a **this month only** target that overrides it.

**A client never sees that button.** It isn't on their page at all — not greyed out, not hidden, simply not there. Their targets are yours and Elize's to set.

**A member setting up their own reports does see it**, and sets their own. It's the same page; the difference is who's looking at it.

There's nowhere else in the tool where a goal gets set, with one exception: **a launch has its own sales goal**, set on the launch itself.

**Two limits worth knowing**, because they explain the next answer:

- A figure can only have a target if there's a right direction for it. "Cash in bank" has no target box, because neither up nor down is the good news.
- A figure can only have a target if it's **one number for the month**. Anything you type in per offer, per campaign or per platform can't have one.

---

## 2. What a client sees where you type

**They see your words and nothing else.** No boxes, no buttons, and no instructions meant for you.

There's a picture of it: `screens-answers/01-client-reads-the-words-desktop.png`, and the same on a phone beside it.

- **Notes from your strategist** reads as your paragraphs, laid out as you typed them, with your name underneath.
- **What we're focusing on next month** reads as a short numbered list. The line you see when writing it — "Up to three. The client sees them once this month is published" — is only ever on your screen.
- Where you have a **Save** button, they have nothing. Where you have a publish control, they have nothing.
- If you haven't written a note, they read one line saying so, rather than an empty card.

This is now checked automatically every time anything changes, so it can't quietly stop being true.

**One thing you'll want to know, because it affects the rename you asked for.** The word "strategist" appears in **five** places a client reads, not one: the card's title, the line under your name, the message when there's no note yet, the sentence above their reply box ("Your strategist reads this with the report"), and the reply button itself ("Send to your strategist"). All five are being changed to the Allegro Team wording.

---

## 3. "Posts published" — how it decides good from bad, and can it have a target

**More is better.** That's how it's set up, so the tool reads a rise as good news and a fall as something to look at.

The coloured dot beside it compares against the first of three things that exist for that figure, in this order: **your target**, then **the benchmark**, then **last month** — and the words beside the dot always say which one it used, because "off track vs. target" and "off track vs. last month" are two different pieces of news.

**It can't have a target at the moment**, and the reason is the second limit in answer 1: posts published is typed in **per platform** — Instagram, LinkedIn, and so on — and a target is a single number. There's nowhere to put one that wouldn't be ambiguous.

Two ways to give it one, if you want it:

- **The small one:** add an all-platforms "Posts published" total for the month, and put the target on that. Half a day.
- **The big one:** targets per platform, so Instagram and LinkedIn can each have their own. That changes how every target is stored, and would show as several bars instead of one.

The same is true of every per-platform, per-offer and per-campaign figure, so this is really one decision about all of them rather than about posts published.

---

## 4. Does "What worked this month" take links to the top posts

**No. It takes words and a view count, and that's all.**

There's no place to put a link, and a link pasted into the text box would be stored and shown as plain text — not something anybody could click.

It's also only on **Trial Reels**, as the top three hooks and the top three b-roll clips. Social Media has no equivalent card.

Adding links is small — a second box on each row and the text becomes clickable. It's worth doing at the same time as the screenshot uploads you've asked about for top posts, because they're the same wish: a way to get back to the actual post.

---

## 5. Exactly how "Still to collect" is worked out

**Total revenue minus cash collected to date.** Both of those are on the launch:

- **Total revenue** is every price option's price multiplied by how many sold, added up — and **payment plans count at the full amount**. A £3,000 offer sold on six payments of £500 counts as £3,000 the day it sells.
- **Cash collected to date** is the figure you type in.

So "Still to collect" is the money that's been sold but hasn't arrived yet. It's set up so a rise is something to look at and a fall is good news, and if either of the two figures is missing it shows a dash rather than a guess.

**The honest limit:** it's one number, not a schedule. The tool knows what's outstanding but nothing about *when* it arrives — which is exactly what the transaction ledger idea you raised would change.
