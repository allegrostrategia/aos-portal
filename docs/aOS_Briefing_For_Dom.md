# aOS — the briefing
*What we're building, and what it feels like to actually be inside it. Read this before the technical brief — it's the "why," the other documents are the "how."*

---

## The one-sentence version

Every month, one real thing that's costing a member time or money gets built, live, based on their own numbers — not talked about, not guessed at. Everything else in this build exists to make that one promise true and to make it feel special while it happens.

## The world we're building it inside

Most membership platforms look like a course library bolted onto a Facebook group — an endless scrolling feed, a folder of videos, a chat nobody reads. We're deliberately not building that. aOS is designed to feel like arriving somewhere, not logging into software — an Italian coastal town, where every part of the business lives inside its own beautifully designed room, connected by a metro map called La Strada. Cinema Allegro for visibility content. Banco Allegro for the numbers. Officina Vespa for automation. Eleven of these in total, each one a genuine destination, not a menu item.

That's the feeling to hold onto while building every screen: **calm, warm, a little bit special** — never a generic dashboard, never a cold productivity tool.

## Walking through it as a real member — meet Sadie

**Day one.** Sadie's just paid. Before she sees a single form, she watches a short recorded welcome from Nina — not the mechanics of the portal, but what this actually is: real builds, real accountability, not another programme where nothing gets finished. Then she gets a beautifully designed itinerary, like a travel document, with her actual dates already on it: when she tracks, when she meets Nina, when her first live session is.

**Then, the audit.** Still early on, Sadie fills in one proper form — not a long narrative essay, a set of multiple-choice questions about her business, station by station. This is what works out which "pot" she's weakest in — visibility, leads, pricing, systems, whichever it turns out to be — and it's also where she gives her actual current pricing and rates, a specific number Nina will need later. This single form is doing real work quietly: it's what tells Claude what to help Nina probe deeper on in the call still to come.

**Weeks two and three.** Alongside that, Sadie starts a daily timer, logging her hours against categories like content, admin, sales calls. She's not filling in a boring form — she's building the exact evidence that will shape her own roadmap. While she does this, a few things quietly keep her engaged: a couple of starter trainings, a peek at real replays of other members' hot seat builds, a channel where people talk about what they're noticing in their own numbers. She's not just waiting — she's already inside something.

**Week four — the reveal.** Sadie gets on a call with Nina. Nina already knows Sadie's numbers before the call starts, so it isn't a discovery conversation, it's a planning one. By the end of it, Sadie receives something genuinely beautiful — a one-off, personalised document: her own honest picture (what's working, what's not), her top three priorities, and her actual road ahead, drawn as a route with stations on it. This is the moment she falls in love with the product, before she's even logged into the portal properly.

**Then she's in.** From the next month onward, Sadie has a rhythm: log her time and her progress each week, show up to one live monthly session where something real gets built with her (not just talked about), get a two-week check-in on how it's actually going. Her home screen — Piazza — always shows her exactly what matters right now: her hours reclaimed, her current focus, her next session, who her buddy is this month. When she wants to explore rather than be told what to do, she can walk the full map herself, wander into any station, and learn something.

**The small delights, on purpose.** Every month, everyone who's kept their logs up goes into a prize draw — styled like an actual raffle ticket, not a banner. Hitting personal milestones (hours reclaimed) unlocks things. The whole community has a shared number they're building together too. None of this is a game with leaderboards and streaks — it's proof, made a little bit fun.

## The two experiences — Standard and Partner

Most members are on **Standard (£350/month)**: the library, the monthly group session, everyone's roadmap moving forward on their own steam, informed by their own data.

**Partner (£650/month)** is the same everything, plus Nina personally, once a month, reviewing and re-pointing that member's roadmap herself, not just the system doing it. Same product underneath — Partner members get more of Nina, not a different product.

## What Nina's side of this looks like

Sadie only ever sees the finished result, but behind it, Nina's workload is designed to stay light no matter how many members join. Before every hot seat, Claude reads what each member submitted and drafts a suggested build — Nina reviews and confirms in a few minutes, she doesn't diagnose from scratch, live, twelve times in an hour. The same pattern repeats everywhere: AI drafts, Nina confirms — for hot seat prep, for the write-up afterward, for the roadmap reveal document, even for members generating their own SOPs. This is the single most important thing to hold onto while building the admin side: **nothing should require Nina's live judgement to scale one-for-one with the number of members.**

## The five things worth remembering while building any of this

1. **One shared template, personal data** — the map, Piazza, every station is identical code for every member. What feels bespoke is the data rendering into it, never a separate file built per person.
2. **Real proof, not vibes** — the hours-reclaimed counter, the handover pack, the pricing/leverage flag — everything points back to actual numbers, never a feeling.
3. **Protect Nina's time, always** — the hot seat is one fixed group slot, not bookable 1:1s. Peer pairing is rotation-matched, not smart-matched. If a feature would grow Nina's time linearly with membership size, it's built wrong.
4. **Calm and warm, not another cold ops tool** — no infinite feeds, no dark-mode dashboards, no gamified streaks and badges.
5. **One motion, many jobs** — the weekly log does time tracking, the challenge, and progress in one submission. Don't invent a new place for a member to remember to visit if an existing motion can carry it.

If every screen gets built with Sadie's actual week in mind, not just the feature list, this comes out right.
